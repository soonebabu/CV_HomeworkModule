Optical Flow, Feature Tracking and Structure from Motion

CSc 8830 Computer Vision, Homework 6. Three parts:

  1. Optical flow video - upload a video, take a 30 s window with motion in it, compute dense optical flow (Farneback) between every pair of frames and get it back as a video: flow vectors drawn on the frame next to a colour-coded flow field. The same page measures what the flow tells us - when and where things move, how fast, in which direction, and whether it's the camera (pan / zoom / roll) or objects moving - and shows the plots and frames as evidence.
  2. Two-frame tracking - pick two consecutive frames, track corner features with a Lucas-Kanade tracker written from the equations (bilinear interpolation included), and check every predicted position against where the point actually is in the second frame. "Actual" is measured independently by template matching, and you can also click points yourself. One feature is worked through number by number.
  3. Structure from motion - four photos of a flat object from four viewpoints. Click the same boundary points in each; the page recovers the camera poses and the 3-D points through plane homographies, estimates the object's boundary in real units, and prints all the matrices along the way.

The derivations (brightness constancy, the tracking equations, bilinear interpolation, the plane-induced homography and its decomposition, triangulation) are on the theory page.

What to capture

Two videos (parts 1 and 2)

  • At least 30 s each, with motion through most of it. Two different kinds of motion make the comparison more interesting, e.g. a static camera watching things move (street traffic, people walking, a fan) and a moving camera (slow pan across a room, or walking forward down a corridor).
  • Keep the phone steady for the static one (lean it against something). Avoid heavy zooming or very dark scenes - flow needs texture and light.
  • Straight off the phone is fine (MP4 / MOV). Processing is done at 480 px, so 1080p is plenty.

Four photos (part 3)

  • A flat object with clear corners - a book cover, a board game box lid, a poster, a laptop lid, an A4 sheet on a dark table. Measure its edges with a ruler.
  • Take four photos from clearly different positions (move the phone around the object, not just rotate it on the spot), with the whole object in frame. Use the same phone and the 1x lens as the HW2 calibration, no zoom, so the calibrated focal length applies.
  • For each photo, note roughly where the phone was (e.g. "40 cm above, 20 cm to the left") - the page compares that with the recovered camera positions.
  • Name the files so they sort in viewpoint order (view1.jpg ... view4.jpg).
