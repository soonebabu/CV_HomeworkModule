Prototype run: baseline vs. Scene-Graph Orchestrator

Write a short story, one sentence per beat (up to 8 scenes). The same story goes through two pipelines that share one generator. The baseline prompts every scene with its own sentence, the way people re-prompt today. The orchestrator plans the story, keeps a persistent world state, and conditions every scene on it.

The offline planner understands simple sentences: named characters ("Mia, a girl with a red jacket", "her dog Max"), objects, places (park, kitchen, beach, street, forest, room, office, cafe), weather, night/sunset, and verbs like walk, sit, pick up, put, open, go. An LLM planner handles free-form text.

Stage 3: persistent world / state graph

Who and what is in the current place after each scene. Edges are relations (held by, on, under, next to), and the log under each graph shows what changed. Objects nobody mentions again stay where they were, so a bench from scene 2 is still in the park two scenes later even though those sentences never name it.

Stage 4a: control adapter

The layout condition per scene (filled box = where it ends up, thin box + arrow = where it starts moving from). Positions are remembered per location, so going back to a place restores its layout. Seeds are fixed per location for the orchestrator and random per clip for the baseline.
