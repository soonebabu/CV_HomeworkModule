Method & metrics

How each stage works, what the stand-in generator is (and isn't), and how every score is computed.

Stage 2: narrative planner

The planner turns the story into JSON: a list of entities (id, name, character/object, kind, attributes) and a list of scenes (text, location, weather, time of day, entities referred to, actions actor-verb-target, spatial relations, explicit attribute changes, exits).

  • LLM planner (when GEMINI_API_KEY or ANTHROPIC_API_KEY is set): the model gets the schema plus the vocabulary the rest of the pipeline understands, and is told to pin down every attribute even when the story doesn't give one.
  • Rule-based planner (offline fallback): splits sentences into scenes, finds named characters ("Mia, a girl", "her dog Max", "a robot named Bolt"), colour + clothing phrases, objects, places, weather words, verbs and relation phrases ("on", "next to", "under", "in front of"), and resolves she / he / they / it to the most recent matching entity.

Every plan goes through the same validator, which drops unknown ids, maps locations and verbs to the known set, and fixes up the shape, so a hand-edited plan works the same way.

Stage 3: persistent world / state graph

A WorldState holds, for every entity: locked appearance, current location (or held by someone), and one spatial relation; plus the environment (location, weather, time). apply(scene) updates it in a fixed order:

  1. Environment changes (a new place, rain, night). Anything the scene doesn't mention carries over.
  2. Explicit attribute changes ("changes into a blue sweater"). Nothing else can change appearance.
  3. Characters referred to move to the current place. Objects appear where they're first mentioned and then stay there.
  4. Actions: pick up / hold / open-an-umbrella / read-a-book attach an object to its holder; put / leave place it; sit gives "on"; walking off clears it.
  5. Relations from the scene ("ball on table"). Carried objects follow their holder to new places.

Appearance the story never specifies is chosen once from a hash of the entity's id and recorded as filled in. The snapshot after each scene is the expected world: who and what should be visible, how they look, and how they're related. The evaluation uses it as ground truth.

Stage 4a: control adapter

For each scene the adapter builds what a conditioned diffusion model consumes:

  • Enriched prompt: setting + scene text + every visible character and object with its locked appearance and rough position, plus a continuity instruction.
  • Negative prompt: outfit/hair changes, extra people, and "missing <object>" for things that must stay.
  • Layout (GLIGEN / ControlNet-style boxes): positions are stored per location, so a scene starts where the last one ended and going back to a place restores its layout. Relations are solved geometrically (on = on top of the support's surface, next to = beside it, under = below it).
  • Identity / scene seeds fixed per entity and per place (an IP-Adapter or reference-image adapter plays this role in a real model), and continuity from the previous clip's last frame (image-to-video conditioning).

The baseline conditioning is just the scene sentence. Attributes, places and objects it doesn't spell out are made up from a fresh random seed every clip, which is how a text-to-video model behaves.

Stage 4b: the generator, and what this prototype does and doesn't show

No GPU is available (on this laptop or on the hosted app), so a diffusion video model can't run here. The offline generator is a procedural renderer with the same input/output contract: conditioning in, frames out. It draws exactly what it's told and invents what it isn't. Both arms use it, so the experiment isolates one variable: how much of the world state reaches the generator.

What this shows: the planner, state graph and adapter carry everything a scene needs to stay consistent, and once that reaches the generator, drift and disappearing objects drop sharply at no cost to per-scene adherence.
What it doesn't show: how faithfully a particular diffusion model follows that conditioning. For that, export the prompt sets, generate real clips, and score them on Evaluate real clips.

Metrics (prototype run)

Measured on the rendered frames. The generator's per-pixel instance maps say which pixels belong to which entity, standing in for a detector/tracker such as Grounding-DINO + SAM2. Colours, positions and relations are always read from the pixels.

AxisHow it's computed
Character consistencyFor every character, the Lab colour histogram of its pixels (middle frame) vs. its first appearance: 1 - Bhattacharyya distance, averaged. The reference resets only after an explicit attribute change in the story. Mean-Lab ΔE is reported alongside.
Object persistenceAverage of (a) objects visible / objects the state graph expects in that scene, and (b) the same histogram similarity for objects. Vanish events (there in scene k-1, gone in k while still expected) are listed.
Spatial consistencyOnly at cuts where the story stays in one place: (a) how far each entity's centroid jumps from the last frame of scene k-1 to the first of k (score 1 - d/0.2 of the frame width), and (b) background stability, 1 - mean |diff| / 60 over pixels that are background in both frames.
Temporal consistencyFarneback optical flow (HW6) between consecutive frames, warp the second onto the first, mean absolute error. Measured inside clips and across same-place cuts; score 1 - error/25 for each, averaged.
Prompt adherencePer scene, checks taken from that scene's own sentence: the entities it mentions are visible, its relations hold geometrically in the last frame, a "pick up" ends with the object at the actor's hand, and the place is right (a nearest-prototype colour classifier on the background).
Narrative flowPer scene, checks taken from the expected world state: the right place, every entity that should be there, each one's locked colour covering a reasonable share of its pixels, and every carried-over relation (held by, on, under, next to).

The overall score is the mean of the six. Character and object identity use the first appearance as the reference, so slow drift counts as much as sudden changes.

Metrics (real clips)

Real videos come without masks or ground truth, so page 2 uses model-free proxies:

  • Temporal: in-clip Farneback warping error, 1 - error/25.
  • Cut continuity: warping error from the last frame of clip k-1 to the first frame of clip k (same-place cuts only when a plan is supplied), 1 - error/40.
  • Appearance stability: HSV histogram correlation of each clip's middle frame with clip 1's.
  • Shared structure: ORB matches between consecutive clips that survive Lowe's ratio test and a RANSAC homography, as a share of 60 matches (capped at 1).
  • Video-LLM judge (optional): 3 frames per scene plus the story and the expected world state are sent to Gemini or Claude, which scores the six axes 1-5 and lists issues.
